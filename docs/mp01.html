Initial Observations

  1. The dataset comprises 11 variables and a total of 61,892 records. All column names consist solely of alphanumeric characters, begin with a lowercase letter, and use underscores to separate words.
  2. Missing values exist in the runtime and views fields. This stems from inconsistencies in the raw data reported by Netflix, specific durations were not recorded for some titles, making it impossible to calculate the corresponding view counts. Missing records also appear in the release_date field, as this information was not always available.
  3. Both hours_viewed and views exhibit extreme positive skewness, sparklines reveal that the data is heavily concentrated near the minimum value. For hours_viewed, 75% of the titles fall within the 6.4 million-hour range, yet the maximum value soars to 964,100,000 hours. Similarly, for views, 75% of the titles fall within 1.2 million views, while the maximum reaches 144,800,000 views.

Corporate Strategy Memo

MEMORANDUM

TO: Netflix Leadership
FROM: Content Strategy & Data Analytics
SUBJECT: Strategic Content for 2027 and Beyond

Executive Summary

This memo outlines strategic recommendations for Netflix’s content strategies for 2027 and beyond. Based on actual viewership data, investment should prioritize episodic series rather than movies. Live event should be used to drive new user sign-ups rather than to build overall watch time. Additionally, the platform must retain classic shows to prevent existing subscribers from cancelling their memberships after finishing new releases.

Data Insights

  • Episodic Series Drive the Core of Viewership

Episodic series are the primary drivers of platform viewership. Since data standardization began in the 2023H2, episodic series have consistently accounted for 94.75% to 96.47% of total watch time across the platform, while movies represent only 3.53% to 5.25% of the share. Since a single season of a series comprises multiple episodes, viewers often binge-watch for hours on end, generating significantly more effective watch time than movies, which are typically completed in one or two hours.

  • Catalog Expansion and Classic Series

Netflix’s catalog has grown steadily, which expanding from 6,574 titles (50,117 total hours) in the 2023H2 to 8,163 titles (62,855 total hours) in the 2026H1, an increase of 24.2% in title count and 25.4% in total duration. Beyond the new blockbuster hits released periodically, the platform relies heavily on classic shows. For example, Pablo Escobar, el patron del mal: Season 1 has generated over 105,500,000 hours of viewership within the recorded periods, despite having premiered years ago. These classic shows are crucial for retaining the audience during gaps between major new releases.

  • The Vast Gap Between Live Event and Pre-taped

While live event generates significant buzz and conversation on social media, the data reveals that most of the viewership remains concentrated on standard pre-taped. On average, pre-taped garners approximately 8,289,547 hours of viewing time, while live event average less than 3,560,000 hours, which means pre-taped generates more than 2.3 times the viewership of live event. The disparity is even more striking when comparing top-performing hits, the most popular pre-taped show reached 964,100,000 hours for a single installment, while the most popular live event garnered only 235,700,000 hours. Once the day of the live event passes, few viewers revisit the replay, unlike standard series, live event cannot accumulate viewing hours over the long term.

Strategic Recommendations for 2027 and Beyond

  • Primary investment toward episodic series IPs

Reduce investment in standalone movies, which often have moderate costs but lose relevance after a single viewing. Therefore, we should concentrate the budget on multi-season shows, as episodic series account for approximately 95% of total platform viewership.

  • Treat live event as sign-up drivers rather than watch-time engines

The primary objective of live event should be to attract new viewers who do not typically watch Netflix, encouraging them to subscribe on event day. We should avoid spending money on small, low-profile live event shows. Instead, invest only in a select few marquee events that draw a massive global audience.

  • Secure long-term licensing for classic shows with high guaranteed viewership

Maintain reliable licensing agreements for multi-episode comfort staples with guaranteed viewership floors. These shows keep subscribers engaged daily between flagship releases, directly minimizing subscription cancellations.

#' Acquire Netflix What We Watched Reports
#' 
#' This function will acquire and standardize all data for MP#01
#' from Netflix. Because Netflix hosts the data files behind a CDN, we
#' are accessing the release pages and automatically extracting links to the
#' relevant files (with CDN hash). 
#' 
#' After import, files are stacked vertically and some basic cleaning is
#' performed. 
load_netflix_data <- function(){
  # Note to students: Make sure you have installed the tidyverse and fs packages
  #                   before beginning this assignment. If you aren't sure,
  #                   simply run `library(tidyverse)` at your R Console and, if
  #                   you don't see an error message, `tidyverse` has been 
  #                   installed. (Same for `fs`) If you still need to install
  #                   either package, use the `install.packages` function as 
  #                   demonstrated in class.
  library(tidyverse)
  library(rvest)
  library(readxl)
  library(fs)
  library(skimr)
  library(scales)
  library(gt)
  
  data_dir <- path("data", "mp01")
    
  if(!dir_exists(data_dir)){
    dir_create(data_dir, showWarnings=FALSE, recurse=TRUE)
  }
  
  NETFLIX_URLS <- list(
    `2023H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-a-netflix-engagement-report",
    `2023H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2023",
    `2024H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2024",
    `2024H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2024",
    `2025H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2025",
    `2025H2` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-second-half-of-2025",
    `2026H1` = "https://about.netflix.com/news/what-we-watched-the-first-half-of-2026"
  )
  
  download_if_needed <- function(url, local){
    if(!file_exists(local)){
      download.file(url, local, quiet = TRUE, mode = "wb")
    }
    
    return(local)
  }
    
  NETFLIX_URLS |> 
    map(read_html) |> 
    map(html_element, xpath = "//a[contains(@href, '.xlsx')]") |> 
    map_chr(html_attr, "href") |> 
    enframe("period", "url") |> 
   mutate(path = path(data_dir, paste0(period, ".xlsx")), 
          path = map2(url, path, download_if_needed), 
          data = map(path, read_excel, skip=3, na="*"), 
          data = map(data, \(x) x |> mutate(`Release Date` = as.Date(`Release Date`)))) |>
    unnest(data) |> 
    select(-url, -path) |> 
    mutate(year=str_sub(period, 1, 4), 
           half=str_sub(period, start=6), 
           period_start = make_date(year, if_else(half==1, 1, 7), 1), 
           period_end = make_date(year, if_else(half==1, 6, 12), if_else(half==1, 30, 31))) |> 
    filter(!is.na(`Hours Viewed`)) |> 
    mutate(live_event = !is.na(Runtime) & str_detect(Runtime, "\\d+:\\d+\\*"), 
           clean_time = str_remove(Runtime, fixed("*")),
           Runtime = 60 * hour(hm(clean_time)) + minute(hm(clean_time))) |>
    select(-clean_time) |>
    mutate(Runtime = coalesce(Runtime, mean(Runtime, na.rm=TRUE)), 
           .by=Title, 
           Title = stringi::stri_trans_general(Title, "Latin-ASCII"),
           Title = str_remove(Title, " // .*"), 
           Title = str_remove(Title, "[^[:ascii:]].*"), 
           Title = str_trim(Title)) |>
    filter_out(Title == "") |>
    select(year, 
           period, 
           period_start, 
           period_end, 
           Title, 
           -half, 
           Runtime, 
           Views, 
           `Hours Viewed`, 
           `Release Date`,
           `Available Globally?`, 
           live_event)
}

NETFLIX_DATA <- load_netflix_data()

NETFLIX_DATA <- NETFLIX_DATA |>
  rename(
    title = Title,
    runtime = Runtime,
    views = Views,
    hours_viewed = `Hours Viewed`,
    release_date = `Release Date`,
    available_globally = `Available Globally?`
  ) |>
  mutate(
    year = as.integer(year),
    available_globally = (available_globally == "Yes"),
  )

### Corporate Strategy Memo

**MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Netflix Leadership  
**FROM:** Content Strategy & Data Analytics  
**SUBJECT:** Strategic Content for 2027 and Beyond

#### Executive Summary
This memo outlines strategic recommendations for Netflix’s content strategies for 2027 and beyond. Based on actual viewership data, investment should prioritize episodic series rather than movies. Live event should be used to drive new user sign-ups rather than to build overall watch time. Additionally, the platform must retain classic shows to prevent existing subscribers from cancelling their memberships after finishing new releases.


#### Data Insights

- Episodic Series Drive the Core of Viewership


Episodic series are the primary drivers of platform viewership. Since data standardization began in the 2023H2, episodic series have consistently accounted for `r percent(min_show, accuracy = 0.01)` to `r percent(max_show, accuracy = 0.01)` of total watch time across the platform, while movies represent only `r percent(min_movie, accuracy = 0.01)` to `r percent(max_movie, accuracy = 0.01)` of the share. Since a single season of a series comprises multiple episodes, viewers often binge-watch for hours on end, generating significantly more effective watch time than movies, which are typically completed in one or two hours.

- Catalog Expansion and Classic Series


Netflix’s catalog has grown steadily, which expanding from `r comma(catalog_2023h2$titles)` titles (`r comma(catalog_2023h2$runtime)` total hours) in the 2023H2 to `r comma(catalog_2026h1$titles)` titles (`r comma(catalog_2026h1$runtime)` total hours) in the 2026H1, an increase of `r percent(titles_growth, accuracy = 0.1)` in title count and `r percent(hours_growth, accuracy = 0.1)` in total duration. Beyond the new blockbuster hits released periodically, the platform relies heavily on classic shows. For example, *`r staple_top$title`* has generated over `r comma(staple_top$min_hours)` hours of viewership within the recorded periods, despite having premiered years ago. These classic shows are crucial for retaining the audience during gaps between major new releases. 

- The Vast Gap Between Live Event and Pre-taped


While live event generates significant buzz and conversation on social media, the data reveals that most of the viewership remains concentrated on standard pre-taped. On average, pre-taped garners approximately `r comma(pretaped_mean)` hours of viewing time, while live event average less than `r comma(live_mean)` hours, which means pre-taped generates more than `r round(ratio_mean, 1)` times the viewership of live event. The disparity is even more striking when comparing top-performing hits, the most popular pre-taped show reached `r comma(pretaped_top)` hours for a single installment, while the most popular live event garnered only `r comma(live_top)` hours. Once the day of the live event passes, few viewers revisit the replay, unlike standard series, live event cannot accumulate viewing hours over the long term.



#### Strategic Recommendations for 2027 and Beyond

- Primary investment toward episodic series IPs


Reduce investment in standalone movies, which often have moderate costs but lose relevance after a single viewing. Therefore, we should concentrate the budget on multi-season shows, as episodic series account for approximately 95% of total platform viewership.

- Treat live event as sign-up drivers rather than watch-time engines


The primary objective of live event should be to attract new viewers who do not typically watch Netflix, encouraging them to subscribe on event day. We should avoid spending money on small, low-profile live event shows. Instead, invest only in a select few marquee events that draw a massive global audience.

- Secure long-term licensing for classic shows with high guaranteed viewership


Maintain reliable licensing agreements for multi-episode comfort staples with guaranteed viewership floors. These shows keep subscribers engaged daily between flagship releases, directly minimizing subscription cancellations.
